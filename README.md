# Student Depression Analysis | Microsoft Excel

## 1. Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing student depression patterns and exploring their relationships with academic pressure, financial stress, lifestyle habits, and other mental health indicators. Using Microsoft Excel, an interactive dashboard was developed to transform student data into meaningful visualizations that support a better understanding of factors associated with student depression.

### Problem Statement

Student mental health is an important concern, particularly as students face various academic, financial, and personal challenges. However, without a structured analysis of student-related factors, it can be difficult to identify patterns and understand how these factors are associated with depression.

### Objectives

- Analyze student depression patterns based on demographic and academic characteristics.
- Examine the relationship between academic pressure and student depression.
- Explore the association between financial stress and depression.
- Investigate lifestyle factors, including sleep duration and dietary habits.
- Identify patterns between depression and other mental health indicators.
- Present key findings through an interactive dashboard.

## 2. Data Source

The dataset used in this project is available on Kaggle:

[Student Dataset – Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/zubairdhuddi/student-dataset)

The dataset contains **6,072 student records** with 17 variables, covering several aspects of student life, including:

- **Demographic Information:** Gender, age, and city.
- **Academic Factors:** Academic pressure, CGPA, study satisfaction, and work/study hours.
- **Lifestyle Factors:** Sleep duration and dietary habits.
- **Financial Factors:** Financial stress.
- **Mental Health Indicators:** Depression, suicidal thoughts, and family history of mental illness.

## 3. Analysis Workflow

The analysis was conducted through the following steps:

1. **Data Understanding**  
   Reviewed the dataset structure and identified relevant variables for student depression analysis.
2. **Data Preparation**  
   Checked data consistency, reviewed missing values, and prepared relevant variables for analysis.
3. **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)**  
   Examined student characteristics and the distribution of depression across demographic, academic, financial, and lifestyle categories.
4. **Comparative Analysis**  
   Explored patterns and relationships between depression and selected variables, including academic pressure, financial stress, sleep duration, and suicidal thoughts.
5. **Pivot Table Analysis**  
   Summarized student records across different categories to identify relevant patterns and proportions.
6. **Dashboard Development**  
   Designed an interactive dashboard to present student characteristics, depression distribution, and key analytical findings.

## 5. Key Insights

**1. Academic Pressure Is Strongly Associated with Depression**

Depression prevalence increases alongside academic pressure. Approximately **91.4%** of students reporting the highest academic pressure (level 5) are labeled as experiencing depression, compared to 24.7% among students reporting the lowest pressure (level 1).

**2. Financial Stress Shows a Similar Pattern**

Approximately **88.7%** of students reporting the highest financial stress (level 5) are labeled as experiencing depression, compared to 44.7% among students reporting the lowest financial stress (level 1).

**3. Suicidal Thoughts Are Associated with a Higher Proportion of Depression Labels**

Approximately **86.7%** of students reporting suicidal thoughts are labeled as experiencing depression, compared to 35.4% among students who do not report suicidal thoughts.

These findings highlight notable associations between student depression and academic, financial, and mental health-related factors. However, the observed relationships should not be interpreted as evidence of causation or clinical diagnoses.
